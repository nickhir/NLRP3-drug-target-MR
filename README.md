# NLRP3-drug-target-MR

Analysis code for the paper *Genetically proxied NLRP3 activity and coronary artery disease risk: a drug-target Mendelian randomization study*.

## Abstract

### Background and Aims

Preclinical studies implicate NLRP3 inflammasome activation, which drives release of interleukin-1β and interleukin-18, in vascular inflammation and atherosclerosis, and selective NLRP3 inhibitors are now in clinical development. However, whether lowering NLRP3 activity reduces coronary artery disease (CAD) risk in humans is unknown. We used human genetics to estimate this effect and its mediating pathways.

### Methods

A genetic NLRP3 activity score was derived by combining the effects of eight cis-NLRP3 variants on NLRP3 expression and three inflammatory biomarkers: C-reactive protein, glycoprotein acetylation, and neutrophil count. The score was validated against circulating interleukin-1β and interleukin-18. Drug-target Mendelian randomization was used to estimate its association with CAD, cardiometabolic traits and other diseases studied in NLRP3 inhibitor trials. Mediation by cardiometabolic risk factors was quantified using the product-of-coefficients method.

### Results

A lower genetically proxied NLRP3 activity score was associated with lower circulating interleukin-1β and interleukin-18, but with higher CAD risk (OR = 1.21, 95% CI 1.02–1.45, P = 0.032). It was also associated with higher systolic blood pressure, apolipoprotein B levels, and type 2 diabetes risk, which together mediated an estimated 87% of the association with CAD. The residual direct association with CAD was attenuated (OR = 1.03, 95% CI 0.85–1.24, P = 0.79). In contrast, a lower score was nominally associated with lower risk of gout, pericarditis, and Parkinson’s disease.

### Conclusions

Lifelong lower genetically proxied NLRP3 activity was associated with higher CAD risk, largely mediated by adverse cardiometabolic effects, with no evidence of protection after accounting for them. These findings do not provide genetic support for lowering NLRP3 activity to reduce CAD risk.
